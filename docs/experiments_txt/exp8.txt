Theory
Named Entity Recognition (NER) is the process of identifying and classifying key information (entities) in text into predefined categories
such as PERSON, ORGANIZATION, LOCATION, DATE, MONEY, and PERCENT. It builds directly on top of POS tagging and chunking — while
chunking groups words into generic phrases like Noun Phrases, NER goes a step further by labeling what kind of real-world thing that phrase
refers to.
NLTK provides a built-in NER chunker, ne_chunk(), which works on POS-tagged text and outputs a tree where recognized entities appear as
labeled subtrees (e.g., PERSON, ORGANIZATION, GPE for geopolitical entities). While functional, NLTK's built-in NER is rule/statistics-based
and can be limited in accuracy on modern text.
For more accurate results, pre-trained deep learning models such as spaCy's en_core_web_sm are commonly used. spaCy directly returns
entities with their text span and label (e.g., ORG, GPE, DATE, MONEY) in a single call, and its output is generally far more accurate on real-
world, unstructured text.
NER is widely used in applications like Information Extraction, Question Answering, Search Engines, and Knowledge Graph construction,
since it turns unstructured text into structured, categorized information.

Aim
Perform chunking for the given text input.
Theory
Chunking, also known as shallow parsing, is the process of grouping individual tokens (words) into higher-level phrases based on their POS
tags, without building a full parse tree. It works in two main steps:
1. POS Tagging – Each word in the sentence is tagged with its part of speech (noun, verb, adjective, etc.).
2. Chunk Grammar Definition – A grammar (using regular expressions over POS tags) is defined to identify patterns, e.g., a Noun Phrase
(NP) chunk rule: NP: {<DT>?<JJ>*<NN>} This means an NP chunk consists of an optional determiner (DT), followed by zero or more
adjectives (JJ), followed by a noun (NN).
Chunking uses a RegexpParser to apply these grammar rules and extract chunks from POS-tagged text, producing a shallow tree structure.
Application
Named Entity Recognition (NER) as a preprocessing step
Information Extraction systems
Question Answering systems
Advantages
Faster and computationally cheaper than full parsing
Easier to implement and interpret than a complete syntactic parse tree
Useful for extracting specific phrase-level information without needing complete grammatical structure
Limitations
Cannot capture deep grammatical relationships between phrases (unlike full parsing)
Accuracy heavily depends on the correctness of POS tagging
Rule-based grammars may not generalize well to complex or ambiguous sentences
